# Improvement Tactics — the 4 tactics + why the brain reconstructs experiences

Four proven tactics improve any customer experience. They work because of ONE fact
about memory, so understand the mechanism first — then the tactics are obvious.

---

## Why the brain reconstructs experiences {#mechanism}

**The memory of an experience is not its average — it is a reconstruction from a few
Moments.** This is the **Peak-End Rule** (Kahneman): when people recall an experience,
their brain heavily weights the **Peak** (the most intense Moment), the **Pit**, and
the **End**, then averages those — it does NOT integrate every Moment equally.

Consequences that drive every tactic below:
- A single great Peak can outweigh a long stretch of mediocrity.
- A bad End poisons an otherwise good experience; a strong End "reduces the negative
  effects of earlier interactions" (this is why the Imprint stage of the Imprint Loop
  matters — see the `psych-audit` skill).
- **Milestones** (stage starts/ends) are also over-weighted — the brain files them as
  events, so marking them creates remembered structure.
- Fixing the average is a waste. Fix the Moments the memory is built from.

**The memory IS the product.** Two customers can have the identical objective
experience; the one who remembers it better is the one who returns and refers.

---

## Tactic 1 — Mark the Milestone {#mark-milestone}

Celebrate the start/end of key stages so the brain records them as events.

- A stage threshold that passes silently is not remembered as an accomplishment.
- Marking it (a moment of ceremony, a "you did it", a status change) turns a step
  into a memory anchor.
- Best applied to the Milestone identified on the map — usually NOT the very first or
  last step, but the meaningful threshold in the middle ("account set up", "first
  project shipped", "you're in").

---

## Tactic 2 — Elevate the Peak {#elevate-peak}

The brain over-weighs the Peak, so raising the highest Moment has outsized return.

- Find the current Peak on the map, then make it higher.
- Elevate it using the user's **top drives** (from the `user-empathy` skill's research
  — anticipation/hope, sensation/pleasure, belonging/acceptance). A Peak that hits what
  the user most wants lands hardest.
- One deliberately amplified Peak beats many small scattered improvements.

---

## Tactic 3 — Fill ONLY the biggest Pit {#fill-pit}

Fix the single lowest Moment. Do NOT try to fix every dip.

- **Potholes are whack-a-mole.** Filling every small pit is a costly, endless game —
  the effort rarely changes the remembered experience (Pareto Principle: the biggest
  Pit dominates the memory).
- Locate the **single lowest-Charge Moment** and pour the effort there.
- Explicitly LEAVE the smaller pits alone — that restraint is the tactic, not a
  compromise. Spending the budget on the biggest Pit + the Peak + the End beats
  spreading it thin.
- To find the cause of a Pit or a big Dip, look at the step **slightly BEFORE** it —
  the cause usually precedes the visible low. Diagnose that screen with `psych-audit`.

---

## Tactic 4 — Reorder to end HIGH (+ preview early) {#reorder}

Rearrange the sequence so the journey finishes strong and shows value sooner.

- **End on the strongest Moment (Peak-End Rule, Kahneman).** The End is weighted as
  heavily as the Peak, so never let a journey finish on a Dip or an anticlimax. Reorder
  so the last thing the user feels is a win. (E.g. surface the "here's what you
  accomplished" summary at the very end, not buried mid-flow.)
- **Deliver a PREVIEW of value early (Hyperbolic Discounting).** People prefer a small
  reward NOW over a bigger reward later. Move a taste of the payoff earlier in the
  flow — a preview, a quick win, a sample result — so the user gets an immediate
  reason to continue rather than waiting for a distant reward.
- Reorder or SHORTEN: sometimes the best reorder is removing steps between the user
  and their first win.

---

## Leverage play — turn WAITS into progress (Labor Illusion) {#labor-illusion}

Unavoidable waits can ADD Charge instead of draining it.

- **Labor Illusion:** showing the behind-the-scenes work being done makes people value
  the outcome more and tolerate the wait ("analyzing 12,000 listings…", a visible
  progress of real steps).
- Use it to convert a Pit-shaped wait into a Moment of perceived progress.
- **HARD LIMIT:** the work shown must be REAL. A progress animation that fakes work the
  system isn't doing fails the Regret test — run it through `ethics-check` before
  shipping. Faked labor is manipulation, not delight.

---

## The ROI of Delight — framing, not forecast {#roi}

Justify investing in delight, but cite the number honestly.

- Reported figure: moving "good" customers (NPS 5-8) to "delighted" (NPS 9-10) earns
  **~9× more revenue** than nudging "average" customers up (industry study, 2017).
- Use this to make the CASE for spending effort on Peaks/Ends and delighters —
  delighting your already-happy users often beats chasing your unhappy ones.
- **Cite it as directional, never as gospel.** It is one study's aggregate, not a
  guarantee for your product. Say "reported at roughly 9×, directionally" — never
  promise a specific multiplier as a forecast. (G6.)

---

## The improvement workflow (priority order)

1. Root-cause the biggest **Dip** and biggest **Rise** (look slightly before each).
2. **Mark the Milestone** — anchor the stage threshold.
3. **Elevate the Peak** — via the user's top drives.
4. **Fill ONLY the biggest Pit** — leave the potholes.
5. **Reorder to end high** + preview value early.
6. Convert any wait via the **Labor Illusion** (real work only).
7. Rank ideas by **impact ÷ effort**; pick the top 3.
8. Gate anything persuasive through `ethics-check`.
9. **Re-map** (Verify mode) — confirm the Peak rose, the Pit filled, and the journey
   now ENDS high.
