# Empathy questions — Compass & Close-up Questions

Research instruments to understand users **before** you design. **Compass
Questions** find the job-to-be-done and behavior levers across a whole audience;
**Close-up Questions** read what's in a user's head on one specific screen.

> **Video > survey.** One live video chat ≈ 100 surveys — you see hesitation,
> tone, and the story behind the words. Surveys scale; interviews get depth.
> Default to video/voice; fall back to a survey only when you can't get calls.
> Target **5 answers per question** — enough to hear a pattern, cheap to gather.

---

## Compass Questions {#compass}

Ask **each** of the three, to **5 people**, about the target behavior `X`
(the job the user is trying to get done). Substitute the real verb/outcome for
`X` and `Y`. Use the patterns **verbatim** — the wording is doing work.

1. **Hope** — surfaces the desired outcome / magic-wand JTBD:
   > "If you had a magic wand and could instantly **X**, how would that change your life?"
2. **Pain** — surfaces the #1 friction and its emotional weight:
   > "What's your **#1 challenge** when it comes to **X**, and why is it so challenging?"
3. **Barrier** — surfaces the real drop-off, told as a story:
   > "Tell me about the **last time** you did **X**. How did it go? What prevented you from **Y**?"

**Why these three:** Hope maps to the Anticipation drive lever, Pain maps to
Sensation (avoid-pain) and to Ease friction, Barrier reveals the exact moment
the user fell below the action line — the highest-value input for an Action Map
diagnosis and a Story Strip.

### Pro tip — ask for a story, not an answer (+300%)

Append this line to any Compass Question:
> "Be super specific to help us understand. **Tell us a story if possible.**"

This lifted response length by **up to 300%**. The Barrier question (#3) is the
strongest story-elicitor — a concrete "last time…" memory carries emotion,
sequence, and cause, which is exactly what feeds an accurate Story Strip and
Action Map.

### Worked example — a same-day "rent a pool" product

- `X` = "book a private pool for this afternoon", `Y` = "complete the booking".
- **Hope:** "If you had a magic wand and could instantly have a private pool for the afternoon, how would that change your day?"
- **Pain:** "What's your #1 challenge when you try to find a pool to rent for a few hours, and why is it so challenging?"
- **Barrier:** "Tell me about the last time you looked to rent a pool. How did it go? What stopped you from actually booking one? Be super specific — tell us a story if possible."

Answers you might hear (5 people) cluster into levers: "figured it'd be $300+"
→ **Anticipation/Money** friction (wrong price anchor); "never heard of the
brand" → **Belonging/trust** barrier; "found you Googling on a hot day" →
**Implicit cue** (situation) already firing. Those clusters become the top-3
drive levers and scarcest Ease lever in the Design-research mode.

## Close-up Questions {#closeup}

Once you have real Compass-Question answers **and** a specific screen, Close-up
Questions read the user's mind *on that screen* — the raw material for a Charge
analysis (see the `psych-audit` skill). Never run Close-up Questions before the
Compass Questions: a screen critique with no audience empathy is opinion.

Ask, for the chosen screen, of both **successful** users and **drop-out** users:

- "When you landed here, what did you **notice first**?"
- "What were you **hoping to see** at this point?"
- "What made you **hesitate** or feel unsure here?" *(blockers)*
- "What gave you **confidence** to keep going?" *(enablers)*
- "If this screen could do one thing better for you, what would it be?"

Interview **both cohorts** — dropouts explain the pit, successful users explain
what carried them over it. One-sided samples hide the real friction.

### From Close-up answers → hand-off

- Blockers map to **friction** badges (red) in a Charge analysis.
- Enablers map to **drive** badges (green).
- The "hoping to see" gap is often the biggest single Dip — hand it to the
  `journey-map` skill as a Pit to fill.

## Common mistakes

- **Leading the witness.** "Don't you find X frustrating?" plants the answer.
  Keep the Compass patterns open; let the user supply the emotion.
- **Asking the future.** "Would you use a feature that…?" predicts nothing.
  The Barrier question asks about the **actual last time** — real behavior, not
  speculation.
- **N = 1.** One vivid interview is a hypothesis, not a finding. Get 5.
- **Surveying when you could call.** A survey can't ask "why?" three times.
